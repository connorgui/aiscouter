GET BETTER RESULTS

Practical AI skills.

The tool matters, but the way you brief, verify, and refine the work matters more.

01

Brief the outcome

Say who the work is for, what success looks like, the required format, and the constraints.

Try this

“Create a one-page launch brief for a nontechnical owner. Include risks, timeline, and next actions.”

02

Give useful context

Include examples, source material, brand voice, and what has already been attempted.

Avoid

Uploading confidential material unless the tool and account are approved for it.

03

Ask for uncertainty

Tell the AI to separate facts, assumptions, and open questions. Request sources when facts matter.

Try this

“Flag anything you could not verify and put sources beside each important claim.”

04

Review like an editor

Check the output against your original goal, correct errors, and ask for one focused revision at a time.

Remember

You own the final decision. AI output is a draft, not proof.

LEARN THE LANDSCAPE

AI field guide.

Plain-language guidance for choosing tools without getting lost in the terminology.

Generalist

A broad assistant that can write, analyze, code, reason, and often work with files or images.

Specialist

A focused product built for one workflow, such as research, coding, video, voice, or design.

Context window

The amount of text and other information a model can consider in one interaction.

Hallucination

A confident-sounding output that is unsupported, invented, or incorrect.

Multimodal

A system that can work with more than text, such as images, audio, video, or files.

Agent

An AI system that can plan steps and use tools to complete more of a task on your behalf.
